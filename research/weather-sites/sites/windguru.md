## What it shows, and how

A colour-coded table for a spot (Guincho was read and rendered): live readings from nearby stations first, then columns of hours and rows for wind, gust, direction, wave, period, rain; cell colour is the value; one table per model with its run time.

Forms: `timeline-hourly`, `models`.

**Seen in a browser (23:48 UTC, 2026-11-10):** Guincho: a "Live Wind" strip of nearby stations with their current reading first (Cabo Raso, Cascais), then the table: columns every two hours for a week, rows for wind speed, gusts, direction, wave, period, temperature, cloud, rain, every cell coloured by its value; the same table repeated per model below, with the model run time ("init") on each.

## What it does best

The heat-map table: forty hours of six variables readable at a glance.

## What we take from it

Colour the cells of our hour strip by threshold (IPMA's yellow/orange/red gust criteria).

## Data and models it says it uses

Named on the page per table: GFS 13 km, GFS-Wave 16 km, IFS-HRES 9 km, IFS-WAM 9 km, ICON 7 and 13 km, WRF 3 and 9 km, Zephr-HD 2.6 km, GDPS 15 km and others, each with its run time ("init"); live readings from named local stations (seen in the rendered page).
