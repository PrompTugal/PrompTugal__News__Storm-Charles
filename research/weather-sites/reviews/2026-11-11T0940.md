## What a stranger misses, at this minute

1. **That the wind was lowered.** Every outlet read at 09:39 leads with it (RTP: "IPMA prevê desagravamento das condições de vento mas mantém-se gravidade da precipitação"; Observador: "IPMA reduz nível de alerta em Lisboa, Setúbal e Beja"; TSF: "apenas Faro está sob aviso vermelho para vento"). Our first screen shows the result (orange wind) but not the change. A reader arriving from those headlines looks for one line: "Wind: lowered by IPMA from red to orange, sent 07:16 UTC", which the CAP record holds as fact (the message and the one it replaces).
2. **The morning has gone from the strip.** The rain row begins at 07:17 (the start IPMA's open data now gives the orange warning, which was in force from 00:00), and the wind row shows no trace of the red that stood until 07:16. The strip reads the current file, so the day's history is rewritten each time IPMA re-issues. The CAP record keeps every message: the past hours could be drawn from it, faded, with a mark where a warning was replaced.
3. **No measurement on the first screen of a desk** while the red rain is in force. At 05:40 the tiles were there (three of four stale); now none is visible until a scroll. One figure beside the banner (rain in the last hour at the wettest Lisbon station, with its age) would answer "is it raining hard now?" as BBC and Yr do.
