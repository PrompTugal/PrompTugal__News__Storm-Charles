# Round 70: a better flights map, planes that move, the list, and how it is worked out

> Kept by incident.designer@web-summit.dinis, 11 November 2026, 23:45 UTC

Dinis (~23:38, BRIEF.md, through the conductor at 23:40) asked for a better map, planes that move, a view of the planes we track, and a visual explanation of how the plane data is worked out. All four are in `app/design/flightmap/flightmap.js` and the `.fm-` rules of `app/site.css` (round 70 block). incident.flights@ owns the data and is widening the areas, polling more often and writing tracks and `how.json`; the map takes those when they land.

**1. The base map.** One file per area beside the app, no outside request: `base-lisbon.json` (23 KB) and `base-madeira.json` (4 KB), clipped from Natural Earth 1:10m (public domain; each source file's URL and sha256 in the file): land, urban areas, rivers (the Tejo, Sado, Sorraia, Zêzere), district lines, coast, and place labels (Lisbon, Setúbal, Santarém, Leiria and Funchal from Natural Earth; Cascais and Porto Santo as approximate label anchors, Cascais at the point incident.datascience@'s tides.json reads). A graticule every 0.25°, a scale bar, north, the runways from OurAirports via incident.flights@'s `runways.json`. The sea is a tint of the water token; greys elsewhere (IPMA's colours stay for IPMA's levels). The clipped base replaces `coast.json` and `coast-madeira.json`. The data box is wider than the view (Lisbon −10.5 to −8.0 E, 37.8 to 39.8 N), ready for wider areas.

**2. Motion.** Each aircraft's measured positions across the snapshots make its track. Play now runs time smoothly from one step to the next (900 ms a step); between two measured positions no more than 15 minutes apart a plane moves on a straight line. **Fact and hypothesis never look alike:** a measured position is a solid dot and a filled plane; our straight line is dashed and a plane on it is outlined; a key under the map says so, the caption counts how many are "drawn between two measured positions", the list tags them "between", and the panel says "not a measurement". The trail is the last 8 measured positions within 45 minutes. Planes turn to their reported track (or, without one, the bearing to the next point).

**3. The aircraft we track.** A table under each map: callsign, airline (ICAO code), type, altitude, speed, heading, phase (from the reported vertical rate: above +300 ft/min climbing, below −300 descending; on the ground from the file), last seen. A row lights its plane and track; a plane opens in the reader's panel (`stormPanel.show`) with its measured positions and, when it is between two, the two times.

**4. "How is this worked out?"** A button beside the time opens a top-down diagram in the reader's panel: adsb.lol receivers → our poll → airline filter → snapshots → tracks → the map (the last box dashed, "our drawing"); Open-Meteo → arrows; METAR → runway heading → the arithmetic, then **the worked example at 14:00 UTC on 11 November, runway 02, from the file's own numbers**: wind 190° at 29 kt gusting 40; runway 02 heads 22°; angle 168°; crosswind 29 × sin 168° = 6.0 kt; headwind 29 × cos 168° = −28.4 kt (a tailwind of 28.4); the file says 6 and −28.4. When `flights/how.json` exists the diagram reads its steps instead (`chains[]` of `{title, steps[{label, what, source, kind}]}`, or a flat `steps[]`).
