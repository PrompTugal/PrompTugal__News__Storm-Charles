# The launch: who we reach out to, what we say, in what order

> Kept by incident.media@web-summit.dinis, 2026-11-11, 02:37 UTC. **A draft for Dinis Cruz.** From his words of about 02:20 UTC ([the brief](../BRIEF.md)): "a nice launch of this site later today ... planning who we're going to reach out to ... maybe do an outreach email ... everything here can be public ... a lot of this needs to be done via public mediums." **A person sends every message and posts every post; nothing leaves by an agent's hand, and nothing here has been sent.** People appear only in a public professional role, with the public work that makes them relevant; each is reached through the channel they or their outlet publish themselves. No private address or number is kept. Not a warning service: follow [IPMA ↗](https://www.ipma.pt/) and [ANEPC ↗](https://prociv.gov.pt/).

## One decision first: when

Today, Wednesday 11 November, is the day of the storm: IPMA's red warnings for Lisbon run to 21:00 and the country is in an alert situation. Two things follow.

- **Nobody in an operational role is approached while the warnings are in force.** IPMA's and civil protection's press offices come last, after the warnings end.
- **A launch during the storm must not look like a warning service or like self-promotion on a hard day.** Every post below says in its first lines where to go for warnings, and what this site is not.

So the plan has two moments: a **quiet opening** this afternoon in public channels (the site is there, here is the data, tell us what is wrong), and **the launch proper** once the warnings have ended (this evening after 21:00, or Thursday morning). Dinis chooses; the drafts work for either.

## The open letter (a page on the site, and the email)

To be published at its own address and sent by Dinis from his own address to the public contact of each group. Plain text, short.

> **Subject: Storm Charles, as a public record: tell us what is wrong**
>
> Hello,
>
> On Tuesday a storm closed a day of Web Summit in Lisbon. While it happened, a small team kept a public record of it: every warning IPMA issued and when, every decision (the government's, the city's, the organisers', the transport operators'), and who reported each one first. It is at **storm-charles.promptugal.news**.
>
> What it is:
> - **A timeline of decisions**, each with its primary source and the time we read it. Facts, evidence and hypotheses are labelled and never mixed.
> - **A record of lead times**: how long before each warning took effect it could first have been known.
> - **A media watch**: 200-odd articles from 16 outlets, each linked and credited, and who was first on eleven key facts.
> - **Data for anyone to reuse**: every file is served as JSON with its hash.
>
> What it is not: a warning service. For what to do in a storm, follow IPMA and ANEPC. We make no forecast of our own.
>
> Who runs it: I do, Dinis Cruz, with a team of AI agents that I direct. The agents read public pages, write the record and the code, and sign their work; I decide what is published and I send every message, including this one.
>
> What is not finished: some outlets do not let a machine read them, so they are missing, not ignored. Captures of the pages we cite are being built. Parts of the site are rough. One outlet's terms forbid automated reading and we stopped reading it; if yours do and we missed it, tell us and we stop.
>
> What I am asking: look at the part you know. If your by-line or time is wrong, if a fact is mislabelled, if our reading of a forecast is naive: say so, in public if you can. The page **/contribute.html** lists thirteen things a reporter, a specialist or a reader can do right now, and how. Corrections stay visible, with credit as you prefer.
>
> How to answer in public: open an issue on the repository (linked from the contribute page), or reply to the post this letter is attached to. I read them.
>
> Thank you,
> Dinis Cruz

## The order, and the time of day

All times UTC (Lisbon is on UTC). Dinis decides each step; nothing is automatic.

| When | Step | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| Before anything | Dinis reads this page, the open letter and [who we would like to talk to](who.md); edits; says yes or no to each group | a person sends |
| Before anything | The checks: the front page loads on a phone; /contribute.html opens issues; the numbers in the posts match [the tables](../research/media/ranking.md) at the time of posting | numbers move every hour |
| Wednesday, early afternoon (quiet opening) | The Mastodon and Bluesky posts; the open letter published as a page | public channels first; small audiences that tolerate unfinished work; the storm is the news, so the post is short |
| Wednesday, afternoon | Open data and civic tech (group 6); data journalists (group 2) by public posts | they can use the data while it is live |
| **Wednesday after 21:00, or Thursday 08:00 to 10:00 (the launch)** | LinkedIn post; the open letter by email to newsrooms' public contacts (groups 1 and 2), each with a link to its own outlet's page | the warnings have ended; newsrooms plan the next day's follow-ups in the morning |
| Thursday morning | Meteorologists and scientists (group 3) with the analysis page | station data is in; the comparison with the forecast is possible |
| Thursday, after operations stand down | IPMA and civil protection press offices (group 4), with the three questions each | not during the event |
| Thursday | Wikipedia talk page (group 5), by a person in their own name; Web Summit's media team and attendees (group 7), on Day 3 | Day 3 runs on Thursday if the organisers' aim holds |
| Friday | A follow-up post: what people corrected, with credit | corrections stay visible |

## What a person must check before sending

- That the site still says what the letter says it says (the counts, the times).
- That no message goes to an operational address during the warnings.
- That The Portugal News (and any outlet whose terms forbid automated reading) is approached, if at all, as a request for permission and not as "your coverage is in our list": it no longer is.
- That every reply is published only as its author agrees.
