## Why ask at all

This site labels every item fact, evidence or hypothesis. Several things we most want to state are still evidence (reported by the press) or hypothesis (our analysis). The only way to turn them into facts is the body's own word, in public or on the record. Each question below names the item it would settle.

## The questions, by field

Each is written to be answerable in a sentence or two, and says what it would change here.

### Meteorology (IPMA; independent atmospheric scientists)

1. At what times (UTC) were Lisbon's red warnings for wind and for rain **issued**? The open data gives the period each covers, not when it was issued. (Settles: the lead-time table, now part press, part two reads.)
2. Was the track ("between Sines and Cabo Carvoeiro") the main uncertainty on Tuesday, and when did the models agree? (Settles: why the rain warning went red about nine hours after the wind.)
3. The government's statement cites a possible **sting jet** with gusts above 150 km/h. What probability did IPMA attach to it, and was it observed? (Now: quoted via the government; our analysis only.)
4. The airport forecast (TAF) weakened at 23:00 on Tuesday while the red warnings stood. How should the public read a point forecast for pilots against a district warning? (Now: our own explanation, labelled analysis.)
5. How does Charles compare with Kristin, in measured gusts and rainfall, once the stations are in? (Now: press comparisons only.)
6. Who named the storm, on what date, and under which criterion? (Now: not read.)
7. For independent scientists: is there anything in our [analysis](../incidents/2026-11-10-storm-charles/data/weather/analysis.md) that is wrong or overstated?

### Civil protection (ANEPC; Lisbon's municipal civil protection)

1. When was the decision taken to recommend that large events be suspended on Wednesday, and on what criteria (the warning level, the tide, the wind on structures)?
2. Is there a written threshold at which an indoor event with outdoor structures and tens of thousands of people is advised to close, or is it case by case?
3. How many incidents were recorded in the Lisbon district between Tuesday 00:00 and Thursday 00:00, by type? (Now: partial counts via press.)
4. Which public warning channels were used (cell broadcast, SMS, the site), in which languages, and at what times? Did visitors on foreign SIM cards receive them?
5. What would you want an aggregator like this site to do, and not do, during an alert?

### Government (Ministry of Internal Administration)

1. At what time was the recommendation to suspend Web Summit on Wednesday communicated to the organisers, and in what form? (Now: press at 17:53; the organisers' page says the government "formally advised".)
2. Does an "alert situation" oblige the suspension of an event in a private venue, or only recommend it? Who decides?

### Transport (Metro, Carris, CP, Fertagus, Transtejo Soflusa, Lusoponte, IP, ANA, NAV)

1. What service ran on Wednesday between 12:00 and 21:00, and what was suspended, at what times?
2. What wind or water thresholds close a line, a bridge, a ferry crossing or a runway, and are they published?
3. When was Thursday morning's service confirmed, and through which channel first?
4. For the airport: how many flights were cancelled or diverted on Wednesday, and when did operations return to normal?

### Event safety (Web Summit; the venues; practitioners)

1. At what time did the organisers decide, and what was the last piece of information that settled it? (Now: the page was published 19:09; press reported the government's recommendation at 17:53.)
2. What happens to Wednesday's programme (talks, the PITCH semi-final, Night Summit, side events), and to Wednesday-only tickets? (Now: unannounced; PITCH timing in conflict between two sources.)
3. Through which channels, in what order, was the decision communicated (email, app, the website, social)? We read the page at its source 109 minutes after it was published: it sat outside the paths our watch read.
4. For the venues: what is the rated wind limit of the temporary structures between the halls, and what caused the water ingress reported on Tuesday afternoon? (Now: press only.)
5. For practitioners: for an event of this size, what lead time is normal between a red warning and a decision to close a day, and what does good practice say about announcing it?

### Journalists who covered it

1. Is your article's time and by-line right in our list? (Copied from each page's own metadata.)
2. What did you want to confirm and could not, for lack of a primary source? (That list is our research list too.)
3. Would a single public timeline of decisions, each with its primary source and time, have been useful on Tuesday evening, and in what form?

## How a reply is used

- A reply is published only if the sender of the reply agrees, and as they agree: attributed to the body, to a named role, or as background that informs a label without being quoted.
- An on-the-record answer from the body that decides or warns can move an item from evidence to fact; the item then cites the reply with its date.
- Replies themselves are not stored in this repository unless public. A summary, the date, and who agreed to what, are.
